What is the 6-Max Hyper SNG course?

6-Max Hyper SNG is BBZ Poker’s dedicated course for hyper turbo Sit & Gos — six-player tournaments where you start with around 25 big blinds and blinds rise every couple of minutes. With only two places typically paid, the format moves from a shallow early game to a three-handed bubble very quickly, and almost every important decision happens between 25bb and 10bb.

That structure is exactly why hypers are popular. Games are short, you can multi-table a lot of them, and the fields at low and mid stakes are generally soft. It’s also why they punish small mistakes: with so few chips and so little time, an overly wide open, a lazy blind defence or a misjudged bubble shove has an outsized effect on your results.

The course aims to fix that with solver-based ranges, worked examples on real board textures, and a full ICM framework for the bubble. BBZ markets it for players at roughly $7 to $500 buy-ins.

Who is Brandon “mazurite” Rollinson?

He is part of the BBZ circle around Jordan “BBZ” Drummond — the coach and backer behind BBZ Poker, whose staking operation finances a large team of online players — and PokerListings featured him in a short documentary about grinding online poker for a living from British Columbia.

He’s not a household name like some of BBZ’s MTT coaches, but that’s fairly typical of SNG specialists: the best hyper players tend to be high-volume grinders rather than streamers. What matters here is that the course is built by someone who actually plays the format, and it shows in the choice of spots.

What’s inside: the full curriculum

The course is split into nine chapters that follow the life of a hyper SNG from the first hand to the bubble.

1. 25bb early game preflop

The opening chapter lays out raise-first-in ranges for every position from UTG to the button, then covers how the small blind and big blind should defend against each open, and finally blind-vs-blind play. At 25bb, preflop decisions carry a huge share of your edge, so this is the foundation for everything that follows.

2. 25bb early game flop strategy

This is the biggest chapter and the one that sets the course apart. Rather than talking about postflop in general terms, mazurite works through button and big blind strategy on 14 specific flops — from dry boards like K92 rainbow and 943 rainbow to connected, two-tone boards like J85 and 754. You see how both players should approach each texture, which is far more useful than a single “c-bet small on dry boards” rule.

3. 25bb early game turn strategy

Five button turn spots that teach when to keep firing and when to slow down after a flop c-bet, including how specific turn cards (overcards, flush-completing cards, board pairs) change the picture.

4. UTG vs BB

A focused section on what changes when the button isn’t the opener. It covers when the big blind can lead into an early-position raiser, how UTG should c-bet, and how check-raising differs against UTG versus the button. It’s a nice touch — most hyper content ignores early-position spots almost entirely.

5. 25bb early game river bluffing

Five river spots on the button that show which hands make good bluffs on different runouts. Short as it is, this chapter is valuable because river bluffing at shallow stacks is where many low-stakes hyper regs leave money on the table by either never bluffing or bluffing the wrong hands.

6. 15bb early game preflop

Once blinds go up and stacks drop to around 15bb, ranges change significantly. This chapter repeats the preflop structure from chapter one — opens, SB and BB defence, blind vs blind — at the shorter depth, so you can see exactly what tightens, what widens and where shoving enters the picture.

7. Bubble theory

Three videos explain the ideas behind bubble play: ICM fundamentals, risk premiums and future game simulation (FGS). If you’ve never studied ICM, this is a clear, practical introduction. If you have, it’s a useful refresher on why the bubble strategy in the next chapters looks the way it does.

8. Bubble preflop play

The heart of the bubble section. Mazurite breaks down button and small blind strategy for four stack situations — equal stacks, chip leader, mid stack and short stack — then covers big blind defence adjustments, short-stack play in three-way all-ins, and small blind vs big blind battles. Because the hyper bubble happens three-handed every single game, this is the chapter most likely to have an immediate effect on your results.

9. Bubble postflop play

The final chapter covers button c-betting on eight bubble textures, including paired boards and boards like 765 two-tone and AJ2. Postflop bubble play is something almost no hyper resource covers, since most players treat the bubble as pure push-fold. Seeing how ICM changes c-bet strategy is genuinely useful.

What we like

  • Built for the format. Most short-stack training comes from MTTs. This course is designed around the actual structure of a 6-max hyper, which is what you want if hypers are your main game.
  • Serious postflop depth. Around 30 videos are dedicated to postflop play at 25bb and on the bubble. That’s rare for hyper content, which usually stops at preflop charts.
  • Logical structure. The course follows the order you’ll actually face spots in a game — 25bb, 15bb, then the bubble — which makes it easy to study one phase at a time.
  • Thorough bubble coverage. Four stack situations for each position, plus three-way all-ins and postflop bubble play, is a more complete bubble treatment than you’ll find in most courses.
  • Transferable skills. The 15–25bb ranges and ICM concepts carry straight over to late-stage MTT play and satellites.
  • Manageable length. At around 15 hours, you can realistically finish it in a few weeks alongside your normal volume.

What could be better

  • No heads-up section. The course ends at the bubble. Since a 6-max hyper usually finishes heads-up, the post-bubble phase is a real gap, and you’ll need separate heads-up study to round out your game.
  • Few 4- and 5-handed spots. The early-game content is built around six-handed play, and the bubble content around three-handed play. The short-handed phase in between isn’t covered in its own chapter.
  • Mostly button-focused postflop. Apart from the UTG vs BB chapter, the postflop work is centred on button vs big blind. That’s where most hands happen, but other position combinations get little attention.
  • No hand history reviews or live play. The course is theory and solver-based examples. If you learn best by watching a coach play and explain real sessions, you may want to supplement it.

Who should buy 6-Max Hyper SNG?

It’s a strong fit if you:

  • Play 6-max hypers at $7–$500 and want a structured way to improve, from preflop ranges to the bubble.
  • Are a winning low-stakes hyper player looking to move up and close leaks in postflop and ICM spots.
  • Play MTTs and want a focused course on 15–25bb play and bubble ICM.
  • Have limited time and want a fast, high-volume format you can grind profitably.

It’s probably not for you if you:

  • Are completely new to poker and still learning hand rankings and basic preflop concepts.
  • Mainly play deep-stacked cash games, where most of this material won’t apply.
  • Want a heads-up course — the post-bubble phase isn’t covered.

How it compares to other options

Hyper-specific training has always been thin compared to MTT and cash content. A lot of what’s available is either older pre-solver material or preflop charts on their own. BBZ Poker itself also sells a monthly Hyper 6-Max Preflop Charts & Trainer subscription with solutions for six-, five-, four-, three-handed and heads-up play — a good companion if you want to drill ranges, but it doesn’t explain the postflop and ICM reasoning the course does.

If bubble play is your biggest weakness and you also play MTTs, Bubble Mastery by Ryan McEathron is a natural follow-up, covering the bubble from a tournament perspective. For pure hyper players, though, 6-Max Hyper SNG is the more relevant course.
