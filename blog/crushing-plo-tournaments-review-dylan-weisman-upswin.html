This is the backbone of the course and where Crushing PLO Tournaments departs most sharply from standard PLO content. Instead of recycling raise-or-fold cash ranges, Dylan teaches a limp-and-pot preflop system built on custom MonkerSolver work at every relevant tournament stack depth:

If you’ve been carrying NLHE preflop instincts into PLO MTTs, the ranges in this module alone will reshape how you play the first 20 hands of every tournament. The limp-and-pot framework is designed to reduce variance and let you play more hands profitably without bloating pots out of position.

The Limp-and-Pot Framework: Why It Matters

The core innovation in Crushing PLO Tournaments is the limp-and-pot preflop strategy. Most PLO players default to a raise-or-fold approach borrowed from cash games, which forces too many marginal hands into 3-bet pots where they get crushed.

In a tournament environment — where there’s no rake, antes increase incentive to see flops, and stack depths vary wildly — limping a structured portion of your range and potting (raising the pot) with another defined portion is provably more profitable than the default raise-or-fold approach used by most tournament regs. Dylan and his analyst Matt Wright spent over 1,000 hours and five years running the sims to back this up.

This isn’t a gimmick. It’s a fundamentally different preflop architecture, and it’s the kind of edge that compounds across thousands of hands.
