Passive Poker Systems Review (2026): BBZ’s Checked-Pot Masterclass

Ask most players what they study and you’ll hear the same answers: c-betting, 3-betting, barreling. Aggression sells. But a huge share of the pots you play never follow that script – the flop checks through, the big blind probes the turn, or a check-raise fizzles into a check-check turn. These are the spots Passive Poker Systems by Jordan “BBZ” Drummond was built for, and almost nobody else is teaching them.

In this review, we break down what’s inside the course, how it fits into the BBZ Systems series, where it falls short, and whether it deserves your study time in 2026.

What Is Passive Poker Systems?

Passive Poker Systems is the official follow-up to Simple Poker Systems, BBZ’s flagship course. Where that course built the aggressor’s playbook, this one covers the other half of poker: what to do after checks, check-backs, and check-raises in heads-up pots.

The numbers: 10 chapters, 70 videos, and 10 hours of training, with lifetime access including future updates. The official price is $185 on BBZPoker.com. Yes, you read that right – 70 videos. The course is built around short, focused hand breakdowns rather than long lectures, which changes how it studies (more on that below).

What’s Inside

  • BB turn probe betting – full theory plus 9 in-depth hand breakdowns on attacking checked-back flops from the big blind
  • Defending vs probe bets – 8 hand examples from the in-position player’s seat, so you learn both sides of the battle
  • River play after check-call → check-check – the classic “he gave up, now what?” spot
  • River play after c-bet → checked turn – delayed decisions with capped and uncapped ranges
  • River play after check-raise → checked turn – navigating the awkward aftermath of flop aggression
  • Double-checked pots – dedicated chapters for both in position and out of position
  • Versatility module – carrying the same principles across formats and stack depths

The structure is mirrored deliberately: for nearly every line, you study it from both seats. That two-sided approach is the course’s quiet superpower – you don’t just learn to probe, you learn what a good probe defense looks like, which sharpens both.

Teaching Style: Systems, Not Solver Dumps

If you’ve studied with BBZ before, the method is familiar: Jordan turns solver-level complexity into simple decision rules, then hammers them home through volume. With 70 short videos, this course leans even harder into repetition than Simple Poker Systems does. Each hand breakdown reinforces the same core heuristics from a slightly different angle, so by the end the “system” fires automatically rather than requiring conscious recall at the table.

Who Should Buy Passive Poker Systems?

  • Simple Poker Systems graduates – this is the designed next step, picking up exactly where that course leaves off
  • MTT grinders leaking in checked pots – if your stats show low probe frequencies or face-probe confusion, this course is surgical
  • NLHE cash players – passive lines are format-agnostic; every concept transfers directly
  • Players who feel lost on rivers – over half the course is river play after passive sequences, the least-studied street in the least-studied lines

You don’t strictly need Simple Poker Systems first – basic solver literacy is enough – but the two courses are clearly designed as a pair.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Covers genuinely understudied territory – your opponents almost certainly haven’t worked on these spots
  • Both-sides structure: every line is taught from the bettor’s and defender’s perspective
  • 70 short videos make it easy to study in small, consistent sessions
  • Heavy river focus, where mistakes are most expensive
  • Lifetime access with future updates included

Cons

  • Narrower in scope than Simple Poker Systems – it’s a specialist course, not a foundation
  • Assumes familiarity with BBZ’s systems approach; total beginners should start with the flagship
  • Heads-up pots only – multiway passive lines are covered in a separate BBZ course
  • $185 retail is a real investment for a single-topic course

Where It Fits in the BBZ Systems Series

The Systems trilogy covers one complete postflop game plan. Simple Poker Systems is the foundation – the aggressor’s core playbook. Passive Poker Systems handles everything that happens after checks. Pressure Poker Systems with Lex Veldhuis adds the advanced aggression layer: squeezes, geometric barreling, and overbets. Studied together, there’s essentially no common heads-up postflop spot left uncovered.

Our Verdict: 8.8/10

Passive Poker Systems does exactly what it promises: it takes the spots everyone ignores and turns them into a repeatable edge. It loses a little ground to the flagship on breadth – this is a specialist course by design – but that’s also the point. Because so few players study passive lines seriously, the EV per study hour here is arguably the highest in the whole BBZ catalog. If you already own Simple Poker Systems, this is the obvious next purchase.
