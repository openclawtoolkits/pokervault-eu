Red Line Mastery at a Glance

What Is the Red Line in Poker?

Tracking software such as PokerTracker and Hold’em Manager splits your results into two lines:

  • Blue line: money won or lost in hands that go to showdown.
  • Red line: money won or lost in hands that end before showdown, because someone folded.

At the micros a negative red line is normal. Opponents call too much, so bluffing rarely pays and you make your money by value betting. As you move up, the player pool gets tougher, recreational players get rarer, and the pots nobody shows down start deciding who wins. Regs who can only win with the best hand get pushed around by players who apply pressure. That’s the problem this course is aimed at.

The idea isn’t simply “bluff more”. Your red line also depends on how you defend: if you over-fold to 3-bets, turn barrels and river raises, you hand the pot to aggressive opponents just as surely as if you never bluffed yourself.

What’s Inside: The Curriculum

Red Line Mastery is split into 14 chapters. Here’s how the 131 videos are distributed:

Aggression (28 videos)

The biggest chapter, and the core of the course. It moves street by street. Preflop covers 3-betting, 4-betting, cold 4-betting, squeezing, isolating limpers and 5-bet jams. The flop section splits single-raised and 3-bet pots and covers c-betting, check-raising, leading from the BB, raising in position and the under-used flop 3-bet. The turn section is the most in-depth: the double-barrel lessons alone run close to an hour each, alongside delayed c-bets, probes and check-raises against barrels.

Calling (11 videos)

This is where the course earns its claim to be about the red line rather than just “bluffing”. Lessons such as why nobody folds to 4-bets any more, how to call the turn properly, and how to continue against check-raises address the other half of the problem: the pots you lose by giving up too easily.

River Play (22 videos)

The river chapter pairs bluffing with bluff-catching. On the bluffing side you get blocker selection, betting and raising schemes in and out of position, and the classic 3-bet pot lines (bet-bet-bet, bet-check-bet and check-raise-bet-bet). On the defence side, thejericho2 works through calling against block bets, standard sizes and overbets, then against triple barrels and river raises. The lesson on calling river raises alone runs over an hour.

Blind vs Blind (18 videos)

BvB is where wide ranges collide and red lines are won or lost, and this chapter treats it accordingly. It covers barrelling and range protection as the SB, pressure and raising as the BB, and full BvB 3-bet pot strategy for both players, including a single lesson of almost 80 minutes on turn and river play as the BB.

The rest

Shorter chapters cover 4-bet pots, multiway dynamics and exploits against recreational players. These are useful because they show when the aggressive default should be switched off. The mental-game chapter is honest about how draining this style can be, and the review chapter shows thejericho2’s study routine in both PioSOLVER and GTO Wizard. The 12 live-play videos add up to nearly 10 hours of him applying the concepts in real games.

What We Like

  • It’s a full strategy course, not a trick list. Most “red line” content is a handful of bluffing spots. This one covers the whole game tree and links decisions across streets.
  • Both sides of the red line. The Calling chapter and the bluff-catching half of River Play give defence as much weight as attack, which is where many players’ leaks actually sit.
  • Solver-based but practical. Solver outputs appear on screen, but the focus is on the reasons and heuristics behind them. You don’t need to own a solver to follow along.
  • It knows when to stop. Sections on multiway pots, recreational players and “knowing when to deviate” keep the aggression from turning into spew.
  • Serious BvB coverage. 18 videos on blind battles is more than most complete NLHE courses offer.
  • Live plays across sites and formats. Zoom, Rush & Cash and regular tables show the strategy in practice, not just on a solver screen.

What to Consider

  • Not for beginners. The course assumes you already know preflop ranges and basic postflop theory. If you’re new to NLHE, start with a fundamentals course.
  • Cash games only. There’s no ICM or tournament content. MTT players will pick up useful ideas for deep-stacked play, but this isn’t built for them.
  • Online 6-max focus. The examples and live plays come from online cash games. Live players can apply the theory but will need to adjust for softer, more passive pools.
  • It’s long. 55+ hours, with many lessons between 30 and 80 minutes, takes real commitment. Plan to work through it chapter by chapter alongside your own database.
  • The full price is steep. At $999 on Run It Once it’s one of the more expensive single courses on the market.

Who Should Buy Red Line Mastery?

  • want a short, quick-win course rather than a long study programme
