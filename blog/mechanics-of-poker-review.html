The Mechanics of Poker Review: Is the TheWakko & Adam Carmichael Program Worth It?

If you’ve spent more than a year watching training videos, copying solver outputs, and still grinding the same stakes, you’ve probably noticed something uncomfortable: the gap between what mid-stakes players know and what they actually do at the table is huge — and most courses don’t even try to close it.

That’s the gap The Mechanics of Poker (often shortened to MOP) is built around. In this review I’ll break down what the program actually is, who it’s for, what makes it different from a typical Upswing-style course, and whether the price tag is justified.

What is The Mechanics of Poker?

The Mechanics of Poker is a full coaching and mentorship program created by Rene “TheWakko” Kuhlman and Adam Carmichael. It’s the spiritual evolution of their well-known Poker Ambition Coaching For Profit (CFP) program — but restructured so any player can access the framework, not just CFP applicants.

Unlike a traditional video course (where you buy a pack of recordings, watch them once, and you’re done), MOP is structured as ongoing access to a coaching system that covers both the technical and mental sides of professional poker. It’s designed for cash-game and MTT players, online and live, at low to high stakes.

Who’s behind it?

Rene “TheWakko” Kuhlman — Head Coach (Technical Game)

TheWakko has been a full-time cash-game pro for 20 years and has earned well over $1,000,000 playing online. What gives him real coaching credibility, though, isn’t the bankroll — it’s the eight years he spent stuck at mid-stakes himself. He knows the plateau from the inside, and the program is largely shaped around the framework he eventually used to break out of it.

Adam Carmichael — Mental Game & Performance Coach

Adam is a former high-stakes player turned full-time performance coach. He holds a degree in Sports Science and specializes in behavioral psychology — backgrounds you almost never see in poker coaching. He’s spent eight years doing 1-on-1 work with players from 32 countries across every format imaginable. His role in MOP is to make sure the technical work actually translates under pressure.

What’s inside the program

MOP is built around two pillars. Both are central to the program — neither is treated as an add-on.

Pillar 1 — Technical Game, Management & Soft Skills

  • A structured study path so you always know what to work on next instead of randomly bouncing between videos
  • Real EV reasoning — when and how to deviate from solver outputs, not just memorize them
  • Hand-reading, range-building and real-time decision frameworks
  • Game selection, table dynamics and the management discipline full-time pros rely on
  • Soft skills: routines, professionalism, self-review processes

Pillar 2 — Mental Game & Performance

  • Practical systems to eliminate tilt, self-doubt and on-the-fly second-guessing
  • Focus, discipline and routine work drawn from sports psychology
  • Variance and downswing management — keeping your A-game intact when the run-bad hits
  • Behavioral-psychology-backed techniques adapted specifically for poker

What makes it different from other poker courses

Three things genuinely set MOP apart from the majority of training products on the market:

1. It’s mentorship, not a video pack. Most courses are passive content. MOP is structured around ongoing engagement and a study system, which is closer to how serious athletes train than how most poker players study.

2. Mental game is half the system. Most poker training treats mindset as an afterthought — a bonus module tacked on at the end. With Adam Carmichael leading that pillar, MOP gives it equal weight. That alone makes it a different product category from something like an Upswing or Run It Once course.

3. The coaches’ own track record. The Poker Ambition CFP that preceded MOP has a documented history of moving mid-stakes regs into high-stakes wins. That’s a harder claim to make than “here’s a guy who beat NL500 once.”
